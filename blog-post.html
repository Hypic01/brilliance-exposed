Behind the Lens

The Art of Capturing Brilliance

February 2026 • 5 min read

How do you photograph a mind? A camera records surfaces — skin, fabric, the glint of glass. It has no setting for a lifetime of research, a theorem, a patent, a symphony. And yet the assignment of Brilliance Exposed is exactly that: make fifty of the most consequential minds in Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics visible.

Michele Zousmer’s answer begins with silence. Before a single frame is exposed, there is conversation — sometimes an hour of it. The honoree talks about their work, their family, the doors that were closed and the ones they built themselves. The camera stays down. “The portrait happens before the portrait,” she says.

The portrait happens before the portrait. By the time I raise the camera, we’ve already made the picture together.

Black and White, on Purpose

Every portrait in the series is monochrome. Color flatters; black and white reveals. Stripped of it, the eye goes to structure — the set of a jaw, the light in an eye, hands that have spent decades at a lab bench or a chalkboard. It is the visual language of the archive, of history being written — and that is the exhibition’s quiet argument: these faces belong in the historical record.

The lighting carries the concept. Each subject emerges from darkness — one side of the face lit, the other falling away into black. Brilliance, exposed: the metaphor is literal. In the darkroom, an image develops out of nothing. In the gallery, a viewer watches fifty minds do the same.

The Mirror Test

There is one measure of success for each finished portrait, and it has nothing to do with technique. When a young visitor stands in front of it, does something in their posture change? Do they stay a beat longer than they meant to? The portraits are designed as mirrors — and a mirror only works if you can find yourself in it.

Fifty sittings. Fifty conversations. Fifty exposures — in both senses of the word. The series continues, and the light is still being gathered.
